# 02 — State of the US Healthcare Services M&A Market (as of late November 2026)

> Context file for the expert-agent team. Last researched: 2026-11-26.
> **Data caveat:** deal trackers (PitchBook, LSEG, Levin Associates, Scope Research, Bain, PwC) define "healthcare services" and "deal" differently (count vs. value, add-ons included or not, disclosed value only). Never mix sources in a single time series. All multiples below are **indicative ranges** and must be refreshed against PitchBook, Capital IQ, GF Data or the bank's internal deal database before use in client materials.

**Interpretation:** the market is **count-resilient, value-light**. Sponsors keep doing add-ons (smaller, lower multiples, financed off existing credit facilities) while large platform trades are scarce and bifurcated: high-quality, commercially-reimbursed assets clear at full prices; Medicaid-exposed or labor-intensive assets face wide bid-ask spreads. PwC describes buyers making "larger conviction-backed investments" with a "higher bar for investment."

## 2. Key drivers in 2026

1. **Reimbursement and policy risk (dominant theme).**
   - OBBBA (2025 reconciliation law) is projected to reduce federal Medicaid spending by **>$900B over 10 years** (work requirements, provider-tax limits, eligibility redeterminations). Most exposed: behavioral health, HCBS/personal care, maternity, long-term care, safety-net hospitals.
   - **Site-neutral payment** expansion proposals (HFMA cites ~$172B in 10-year federal savings in one package) threaten hospital outpatient departments and change the economics of physician–hospital alignment, while potentially favoring ASCs and independent sites of care.
   - Medicare Physician Fee Schedule conversion-factor pressure; Medicare Advantage rate notices and risk-adjustment (V28) changes affect value-based care enablers.
   - Becker's reports hospitals "selling to survive" ahead of 2027 reimbursement cuts.
2. **Cost of capital.** Fed funds at 3.75–4.00% after the October 28, 2026 FOMC hold (two dissents in favor of a hike); long rates elevated. Unitranche pricing ~SOFR + 450–575 bps. All-in debt cost ~8.5–10% compresses leverage capacity (see `05-macro-rates-and-deal-activity.md`).
3. **Exit backlog.** Sponsors hold large inventories of 2019–2021 vintage healthcare platforms bought at peak multiples. Solutions: continuation funds, minority recaps, partial exits, dividend recaps, and holding longer.
4. **Regulatory scrutiny of PE in healthcare.** FTC Healthcare Task Force (March 2026), the revised HSR form, state transaction-notice laws, CPOM tightening (e.g., Oregon). This raises timeline and closing risk, especially for physician roll-ups.
5. **Labor.** Clinical wage inflation has moderated from 2022–2023 peaks, but staffing remains the main margin risk for home health, behavioral and post-acute; staffing companies themselves have normalized from COVID-era highs.
6. **Public market signal.** Median public healthcare services EV/EBITDA ≈ **11.5x in 2026, down from ~14.5x a year earlier** (FOCUS dashboard). PwC and Baker Tilly note behavioral health, long-term care, home health and hospice were among the stronger public subsectors in H1 2026.

Notes on ranges:
- **Size premium:** sub-$5M EBITDA businesses often trade 2–5 turns below platforms. GF Data and similar MM databases consistently show multiples rising with deal size.
- **Platform vs. add-on gap** is the engine of **multiple arbitrage** in buy-and-build (see LBO section in `04-valuation-methods.md`).
- Ranges are compiled from broker/bank market reports (FOCUS, PCE, Capstone-type publications) and general practice; treat as starting hypotheses, not comps.

## 4. Who buys

### 4.1 Financial sponsors
- **Large-cap healthcare specialists / generalists** active in services: KKR, Bain Capital, Blackstone, Welsh Carson Anderson & Stowe, TPG, Warburg Pincus, Hellman & Friedman, Advent, Apollo (credit-led).
- **Middle-market healthcare specialists:** e.g., Shore Capital Partners, Audax, Varsity Healthcare Partners, Revelstoke, Cressey & Company, Frazier Healthcare Partners, Webster Equity Partners, Linden Capital Partners, Harvest Partners, Kohlberg, Thomas H. Lee (services), Amulet, Comvest, BelHealth, NMS Capital, Trinity Hunt, Riverside, Ridgemont, Gryphon, Sheridan Capital, Vesey Street, Princeton Equity Group, WindRose Health Investors, Petra Capital, MBF Healthcare Partners.
- **Independent sponsors and family offices** — increasingly important in the lower middle market.
- **Credit funds / private credit** are both financing sources and occasionally owners via restructurings.

### 4.2 Strategic acquirers
- **Payers / vertically integrated players:** UnitedHealth/Optum (scrutiny has slowed provider acquisitions), CVS/Aetna (Oak Street, Signify), Humana (CenterWell), Elevance (Carelon).
- **Health systems / hospitals:** HCA, Tenet (USPI in ASCs), UHS, Community Health (divesting), non-profit systems (merging and divesting).
- **Public services operators:** The Ensign Group, Pennant, Addus HomeCare, Encompass Health, Option Care Health, Acadia Healthcare, Surgery Partners, Select Medical, U.S. Physical Therapy, LifeStance, DaVita, Quest, Labcorp, RadNet, AMN, Privia.
- **PE-backed platforms** — functionally strategics; the largest source of add-on demand.

*(Verify current listing status before building comps — several names have been acquired, delisted or reorganized.)*

### 4.3 Buyer mix data
Different trackers disagree: PwC says PE drives most deal flow via add-ons; PCE's LTM data attributes the majority of counted deals to strategics (definitions differ — PE-backed platform add-ons are often coded as "strategic"). **Agents should always state the definition when quoting buyer mix.**

## 5. Outlook (as of November 2026)

- **Base case (consensus-ish):** volume stable to modestly up into 2027; quality assets command full prices; Medicaid-exposed and labor-heavy assets trade at discounts or via structured deals (earn-outs, seller notes, rollover).
- **Bull case (Sikich):** strongest year since 2021 if financing costs ease and policy uncertainty clears.
- **Bear case (Ankura):** a broad rebound in volume is unlikely; rate-hike risk (two FOMC dissents favored hiking in October) and 2027 reimbursement cuts delay platform exits.
- **Watch list:** December 8–9, 2026 FOMC and dot plot; CY2027 Medicare final rules (PFS, OPPS, HH PPS); state Medicaid budget actions; FTC enforcement in physician roll-ups; HY spreads.
