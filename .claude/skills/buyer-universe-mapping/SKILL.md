# Buyer universe mapping

Reference: `docs/02-healthcare-services-ma-market.md` section 4; LBO logic in `docs/04-valuation-methods.md` section 4.

## Checklist for her buyer list

1. **Segmentation:** large-cap sponsors, middle-market healthcare specialists, lower-middle-market and independent sponsors and family offices, PE-backed platforms (add-on buyers), and strategics (payers and vertically integrated players, health systems, public services operators).
2. **Fit test for each name:** subsector focus, check size vs. fund size, existing platform in the space (an add-on fit or a conflict?), geography, payer-mix comfort (Medicaid-averse funds), recent deals or exits.
3. **Ability to pay:** sponsors are bounded by the LBO (`lbo-review`); platforms can pay more when the deal is a cheap add-on to their higher-multiple platform; strategics pay for synergies but face antitrust and regulatory limits (e.g., payer–provider deals under scrutiny).
4. **Sizing:** a targeted auction usually has 10–30 names; a broad one 50–150. Is her list size consistent with the process type?
5. **Missing obvious buyers:** the platforms already rolling up the subsector, and recent acquirers from the precedents list.
6. **Names to exclude or handle carefully:** competitors who want the data room for information, buyers with FTC exposure, funds near the end of their investment period.
7. **Buyer-mix data:** trackers disagree on PE vs. strategic share, because PE-backed add-ons are often coded "strategic". State the definition.

The list itself is a snapshot from November 2026. Ask her to verify current portfolios and fund status rather than supplying them.
