# Process and timeline design

Reference: `docs/01-middle-market-ma-advisory.md` sections 4–5.

## Benchmark sell-side calendar (roughly 5–9 months)

| Phase | Typical duration |
|---|---|
| Preparation (sell-side QoE, CIM, model, buyer list, data room) | 4–8 weeks |
| First round (teaser, NDAs, CIM, IOIs) | 4–6 weeks |
| Second round (management presentations, data room, draft SPA, LOIs) | 6–8 weeks |
| Exclusivity and confirmatory diligence, signing | 4–8 weeks |
| Regulatory and closing (HSR 30 days; state notice 30–180+ days; CHOW; licensure; payer consents) | 0–6 months |

## Checklist

1. **Process type fits the asset:** broad auction (scaled, clean asset), targeted (the middle-market default), preemption or negotiated (an obvious strategic fit), or dual track / minority recap (weak exit market).
2. **Calendar built backward** from the regulatory critical path. Are the state notice periods for the footprint on the calendar? Hand off to `healthcare-regulatory-checklist`.
3. **Sell-side QoE and coding pre-audit** happen before launch, to avoid re-trades.
4. **Buyer list size and mix** match the process type (see `buyer-universe-mapping`).
5. **Process letters** specify what each round must contain (value range, financing sources, key assumptions, diligence needs, structure).
6. **Bid matrix** compares headline value against cash at close, rollover and earn-out, financing certainty, diligence status, regulatory risk, R&W insurance and closing conditions. Picking the highest headline is not enough.
7. **Competitive tension** kept through the LOI; the length of exclusivity is justified.
8. **Holidays and reporting dates:** the data room refreshed with new quarterly numbers, and launches not timed into year-end.

Buy-side plans: thesis and criteria → screening → outreach → valuation and walk-away price → diligence plan → financing → integration. Check that the walk-away price exists and is written down.
